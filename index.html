DropStop: product intelligence for Indian e-commerce

An AI market-research tool that helps Indian e-commerce sellers find products worth selling. It started as a Chrome extension and grew into a full SaaS with 10,000 users.

  • Cross-market arbitrage detection. It reads Amazon US trends, models competition in India (review depth, price compression, ad density), and surfaces the top 10% of products with low competition.
  • A C++ ranking engine via pybind11 that scores products on several signals at once. It gave a 40× throughput gain.
  • 50k+ products from 100+ suppliers, pulled through a concurrent pipeline that keeps working when APIs fail or hit rate limits. Caching cut API calls by 85%, bringing infrastructure cost down to 800 credits a month.
  • End-to-end system design: microservices on GCP Cloud Run, Firebase, OAuth, a Next.js frontend, Flask and C++ services, and scheduled refresh jobs. Integrated the Meta Graph, Amazon Product Advertising and Google Analytics APIs, plus Razorpay with 3 subscription tiers.
  • Team: mentored 3 engineering and marketing interns through agile sprints and code reviews.

    03 — Research

    Mapping how movements spread online.

    Before startups, I studied networks: who follows whom, which nodes matter, and how a protest moves from the street to the timeline. That same lens is how I read markets now.

    Peer-reviewed · Global Policy, 2023

    Influencers as institutions: Impact of digital politics in the Global South

    Sinha, K., Jhalani, P., Khan, A. & Mukherjee, P.C. Global Policy 14(5), 912–924. Earlier versions were presented at SACA-AEJMC and the Centre de Sciences Humaines conference in 2021.

    I collected follower-followee graphs connecting protesters, governing and opposition politicians, journalists and celebrities for #BlackLivesMatter and #antiCAA, using Tweepy, NetworkX and Gephi. Nodes were sized by eigenvector centrality to show who actually carried each movement.
